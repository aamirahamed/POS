## 5. Work — the projects (the proof)

### Hero case studies

**1. Flash Approval — Reliability (PM, reported to CPO)**
Mygate's whole promise is approving a gate visitor in seconds. On certain Android models (Samsung especially) the approval notification arrived late or never — and the failure was invisible (surfaced via ticket spikes, souring NPS, account-manager escalations, and approvals quietly drifting to the security guard = lost ad impressions + eroding trust). Rather than debug from a dashboard, he took it to users: **100+ calls/online meetings** with the engineering manager listening in, proved the failure live on a fully-configured phone, and *priced* it (guard-approved visitor = lost ad; community losing trust = subscription at risk) — which doubled the root-cause effort. He worked with Google and Samsung on the OEM kill-chain, then shipped **Flash Approval** (a bubble/chat-head fallback the OS can't kill), a per-device diagnostics layer, and a months-long proactive rollout. **Impact (vs pre-rollout):** +10% app approvals · −50% notification support tickets · +10% notification NPS · **₹1.5 Cr+ ad revenue protected.** The CPO called the work out internally.

**2. Community Hub — Engagement (PM)**
A dead "community" tab (a grid of icon-card shortcuts, no engagement, zero revenue) that the ad engine needed as inventory. The real problem: everyone had an opinion on what belonged. He ran the discovery properly — power-user interviews, then an **8-person Google Design Sprint** that *killed* the features the team was sure about and surfaced what residents actually wanted: each other. Rebuilt around the survivors, shipped **Find Your Tribe** (find neighbours by interest/profession/hometown — networking, or a doctor two buildings away), and *held ads back for the first months* so a reviving tab wouldn't feel ad-filled. **Impact (30 days):** 2.6× tab impressions · +100% directory usage · 35% CTR on Tribes · +166% ad-revenue *potential* (modeled from new inventory).

**3. Ad Platform Scaling — Monetisation (Program Manager)**
Mygate pivoted to a Community Engagement Platform (hyper-local advertising as a primary revenue driver). After the pilot, the CEO tasked the business-excellence team with making it scale. Every campaign was hand-assembled across five teams. He built **one operating framework across six stages (Capture → Price → Inventory → Execute → Measure → Report)** and seven systems to run it — including the judgment call to bring **guardrails (maker-checker), not a straitjacket** when sales wanted total freedom (overlapping notification ads had collapsed click-through, free "bonus" sends diluted rates, and every rep could pull the full client DB). **Impact: ad revenue ~$0.6M → $12M/year** (a ~20× scale-up, toward a ₹10 Cr/month run-rate). *(Figures beyond the public revenue are treated as confidential; the framework, not the internals, is what's shown.)*

**5. Lease Management System — Execution (PM)**

The wedge: Mygate already owned residents' daily community life ("Live"), so leasing was the natural extension that completes the whole tenant lifecycle in one platform. As PM he owned vision, competitive solutioning, and the PRD; engineers built. **In two weeks** they stood up the full tenancy lifecycle (Onboarding → Project setup → Prospect → Offer letter → Lease → E-signing → Cheque capture → Charges → Move-in → Renewal), localised for the Gulf (Emirates & Saudi ID checks, VAT, multi-currency SAR/AED, post-dated-cheque ledger, RBAC, in-app e-signing, C-suite dashboard). *Honest scope: built and production-ready to pursue an enterprise deal — not rolled out on his watch, no closed-deal claim.*

**7. Inventory & Process Overhaul — Execution (Program Manager, Business Excellence)**

## 7. Recalibrated for AI — how his way of working changed

His headline for the AI shift: **"Building got cheap. Deciding got expensive."** (88% of companies now use AI; only ~6% get real value — the gap isn't the building, it's the deciding.) Framed as a personal changelog:

- **Deprecated — ten-page PRDs.** Replaced by working prototypes people can click, react to, and argue with (engineers still build the production version — he builds to learn, not to replace them).
- **New — eval sets.** "Good" is defined and tested before anything AI ships; where AI belongs is a trade-off call (accuracy vs trust, latency vs cost), not a default.
- **Improved — discovery speed.** The busywork (drafting, clustering feedback, root-causing) goes to AI; his week goes to what to build and what to cut. When building gets cheap, the scarce skill is refusal.
- **Unchanged — trust, reading the room, systems that survive reality.** AI can't earn trust, read hesitation, or untangle a political process — that stays human (it's how Mygate's ad model scaled $0.6M → $12M).

His one-line: **"AI is the multiplier. Judgment is what it multiplies."**
